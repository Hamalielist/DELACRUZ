Introduction

The Philippines is a country of more than 7,000 islands, with mountains, lagoons, waterfalls, and centuries-old towns. Here are five places every traveler should see.

Banaue Rice Terraces

Built by the Ifugao people about 2,000 years ago, these terraces climb the Cordillera mountains in steep, stepped layers. They are often called the "Eighth Wonder of the World" and are best seen during planting season, when the fields glow bright green.

Watch: Banaue

El Nido, Palawan

El Nido sits on the northern tip of Palawan and is known for its dramatic limestone cliffs, secret lagoons, and white beaches. Island-hopping tours take visitors to spots like the Big Lagoon and Small Lagoon, which are ideal for kayaking and snorkeling.

Watch: El Nido

Coron, Palawan

Coron is famous for its crystal-clear lakes, warm lagoons, and World War II shipwrecks resting on the seabed. Divers come to explore the wrecks, while others swim in Kayangan Lake and soak in the natural hot springs at Maquinit.

Watch: Coron

Kawasan Falls, Cebu

Kawasan Falls is a multi-tiered waterfall deep in the jungle of Badian, in southern Cebu. Its bright turquoise water makes it perfect for swimming, bamboo rafting, and canyoneering, a popular activity that leads visitors down the river to the falls.

Watch: Kawasan Falls

Vigan, Ilocos Sur

Vigan is one of the best-preserved Spanish colonial towns in Asia and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visitors can ride a horse-drawn kalesa down Calle Crisologo, admire the old stone mansions, and taste local favorites like empanada and longganisa.

Watch: Vigan
